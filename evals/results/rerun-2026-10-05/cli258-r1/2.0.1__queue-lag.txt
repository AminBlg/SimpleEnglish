Consumer lag of 2 million means your consumers are 2 million messages behind the producers — the queue is growing faster than it is being drained. Whether that is bad depends on your message rate and the processing time budget each message has.

If your producers write 10,000 messages per second and your consumers process 9,000, lag grows by 1,000 per second, so 2 million represents roughly 33 minutes of backlog. If each message drives a user-visible action (a notification, an order status update, a payment confirmation), users experience that same delay. If messages are purely internal analytics, users notice nothing.

The real risk is that lag keeps growing rather than stabilizing. A stable high-lag number means consumers are slow but keeping up at a plateau; a number that climbs continuously means you have a runaway backlog and must add consumer instances or reduce producer throughput before you hit retention limits and start losing messages entirely.
