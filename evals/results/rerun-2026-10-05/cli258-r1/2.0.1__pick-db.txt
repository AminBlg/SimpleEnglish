Before sharding, add indexes on your query predicates — that alone often drops read latency by orders of magnitude on a 40M-row table.

Run `EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, BUFFERS)` on your slowest queries to see whether you have sequential scans or index misses. Partial indexes, composite indexes in the right column order, or a covering index (one that includes all projected columns) can eliminate the problem without any architecture change.

If indexed reads are still slow, the next step is partitioning (range or list on a natural key like `created_at` or `tenant_id`), which Postgres supports natively and is far cheaper operationally than sharding. Sharding — splitting data across multiple Postgres instances — introduces distributed transaction complexity, cross-shard query fan-out, and operational overhead that is rarely justified under a few hundred million rows.

Only reach for sharding if you have exhausted indexes, partitioning, connection pooling (PgBouncer), and read replicas.
