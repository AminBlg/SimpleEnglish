**Yes, 2 million lag is serious — here's what it means:**

## What consumer lag actually is

Lag = messages produced but not yet consumed. At 2M lag, your consumer is behind the producer by 2 million messages.

Whether that's critical depends on two things:

1. **Throughput** — if your consumer processes 1M msg/s, 2M lag = 2 seconds behind. If it processes 10K msg/s, that's 200 seconds and growing.
2. **Trend** — is it stable, growing, or shrinking? A stable lag at 2M is a different problem than one that climbed from 500K in the last hour.

## What users feel

| Lag behavior | User impact |
|---|---|
| Stable high lag | Consistent delay (e.g., notifications always arrive 30s late) |
| Growing lag | Increasing latency — eventually events stop arriving at all |
| Lag spike + recovery | Temporary delay users notice, then resolves |

If your pipeline is real-time (payments, alerts, live feeds), 2M growing lag likely means **users are seeing stale data or missing events entirely**. If it's async/batch (analytics, audit logs), impact is lower but you're heading toward retention cutoff risk — Kafka will delete old messages before they're consumed.

## Immediate questions to ask

- **Is lag growing or stable?** (`kafka-consumer-groups.sh --describe` or your dashboard trend)
- **Is the consumer healthy?** — check for rebalancing, GC pauses, or thread starvation
- **Is the producer bursting?** — sudden traffic spike vs. consumer falling over are different fixes
- **What's your retention window?** — if messages expire before consumption, they're gone permanently

## Common causes

- Consumer crashed or scaled down
- Processing bottleneck (slow DB writes, downstream service timeout)
- Partition imbalance (one consumer thread doing all the work)
- A recent deploy slowed consumer throughput

The most urgent thing is determining the **rate of change** — if lag is growing at even 10K/min, you have maybe 3 hours before hitting typical 24h retention limits. What does your trend line look like?
