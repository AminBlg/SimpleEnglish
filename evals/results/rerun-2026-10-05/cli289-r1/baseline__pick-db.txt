Before sharding (which is complex and often overkill), work through this checklist in order:

## 1. Diagnose first

```sql
-- Find slow queries
SELECT query, mean_exec_time, calls, total_exec_time
FROM pg_stat_statements
ORDER BY mean_exec_time DESC LIMIT 20;

-- Check table bloat and index usage
SELECT schemaname, tablename, n_live_tup, n_dead_tup, last_vacuum, last_analyze
FROM pg_stat_user_tables
WHERE tablename = 'your_table';

-- Check which indexes exist and are actually used
SELECT indexrelname, idx_scan, idx_tup_read
FROM pg_stat_user_indexes
WHERE relname = 'your_table';
```

## 2. Low-effort wins (do these first)

- **VACUUM ANALYZE** — dead tuples cause bloat and slow scans
- **Missing indexes** — most "slow reads" on large tables are just sequential scans that a composite index would fix
- **`EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, BUFFERS)`** on your slow queries — confirm whether it's a seq scan, a bad join, or an index that exists but isn't being used
- **`work_mem`** — bumping it (e.g. `SET work_mem = '256MB'`) for sort/hash-heavy queries can be dramatic

## 3. Medium-effort wins

- **Read replicas** — if you're read-heavy, a streaming replica handles reads with zero schema changes
- **Partial indexes** — if queries always filter by status, created_at range, etc., a partial index is tiny and fast
- **Table partitioning** (Postgres native) — partition by date or another natural dimension; this is often 80% of what people actually need from "sharding" with far less complexity

## 4. Sharding — only if

- You've exhausted the above and reads are still slow
- You're write-bottlenecked (sharding helps writes; read replicas are usually better for reads)
- Your dataset genuinely can't fit on one machine even with SSD

**Sharding with Postgres is operationally painful.** Citus (now part of Azure) or Vitess are the practical paths if you get there. Most 40M-row tables don't need it — that's not big by Postgres standards.

**Start with `EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, BUFFERS)` on your slowest query.** That output will tell you exactly what's wrong.
