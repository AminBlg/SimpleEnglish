**Yes, 2 million lag is bad** — but how bad depends on your throughput.

## What it means

Consumer lag = messages produced but not yet consumed. At 2M lag, your consumers are behind by 2 million unprocessed messages.

**The key metric is time-to-drain, not the raw number:**
- At 100k msg/s throughput → 20 seconds behind (probably fine)
- At 1k msg/s throughput → 33 minutes behind (likely a problem)
- If consumers are slower than producers → lag grows indefinitely (crisis)

## User impact depends on your pipeline

| Use case | Impact at 2M lag |
|---|---|
| Real-time notifications | Users see delayed alerts/messages |
| Event-driven DB writes | Stale reads, data appears "missing" |
| Analytics/metrics | Dashboards show outdated data |
| Async background jobs | Jobs queue up, SLAs breached |
| Log aggregation | Usually tolerable |

## What to check right now

1. **Is lag growing or stable?** — `kafka-consumer-groups.sh --describe` — growing = active incident, stable = backpressure you can drain
2. **Consumer throughput** — are consumers processing at all, or stuck/crashed?
3. **Producer rate vs consumer rate** — if producers outpace consumers, adding consumer instances (if the topic has enough partitions) is the fastest fix
4. **Topic partition count** — max parallelism is capped at partition count; if you have 4 partitions, a 5th consumer does nothing

## Immediate actions

- Check if consumers are alive and processing (no errors, no rebalancing loops)
- Scale out consumer instances up to partition count
- If lag is growing: treat as incident — users are likely experiencing delays proportional to the drain time

What's your consumer group's current throughput and partition count? That'll tell you if you're looking at minutes or hours of backlog.
