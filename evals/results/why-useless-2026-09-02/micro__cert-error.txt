The error means curl cannot verify the server's SSL certificate because it does not recognize the certificate authority (CA) that signed it — your internal API likely uses a private or self-signed CA that is not in curl's default trust store.

To fix it properly, get your organization's root CA certificate (a `.crt` or `.pem` file, often distributed by your IT or platform team) and pass it to curl with `--cacert /path/to/internal-ca.crt`. If you want to add it permanently, append the CA certificate to your system's CA bundle (on Debian/Ubuntu: `/etc/ssl/certs/ca-certificates.crt`; on RHEL/Arch: `/etc/pki/tls/certs/ca-bundle.crt`) and run the system update command (`update-ca-certificates` or `update-ca-trust`). A quick but insecure workaround is `curl -k` (which disables verification entirely), but do not use that in scripts or production because it removes all protection against man-in-the-middle attacks.
