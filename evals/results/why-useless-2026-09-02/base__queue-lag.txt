**Yes, 2 million consumer lag is almost certainly bad** — though the severity depends on context.

## What it means

Consumer lag = the gap between the latest message produced to a topic and the latest message your consumer has processed. A lag of 2 million means your consumers are 2 million messages *behind* producers right now.

## Why it matters for users

The practical impact depends on **how fast messages are produced** (the lag-to-drain time):

| Production rate | Time to drain 2M lag | User impact |
|---|---|---|
| 100k msg/sec | ~20 seconds | Probably fine, temporary spike |
| 10k msg/sec | ~3 minutes | Noticeable delays |
| 1k msg/sec | ~33 minutes | Serious degradation |
| Growing lag | Never drains | System is falling behind — escalating problem |

**If the lag is growing** (not stable or shrinking), that's the real alarm: consumers can't keep up with producers and will never self-recover without intervention.

Common user-visible symptoms:
- **Delayed events** — notifications, order updates, feed changes arrive late
- **Stale reads** — dashboards or views showing outdated state
- **Queue buildup** — downstream services starved of data

## What to check first

1. **Is lag growing, stable, or shrinking?** — Stable at 2M is very different from climbing.
2. **Consumer CPU/memory** — Are consumers saturated?
3. **Rebalancing** — Frequent rebalances stall processing.
4. **Partition count vs. consumer count** — Consumers can't exceed partition count; you may need to scale out.
5. **Slow deserialization or DB writes** — Per-message processing time may have regressed.

The number alone isn't the whole story — **trend over time** is what determines urgency. If it's been flat at 2M for hours with no user complaints, it may be a batch workload operating normally. If it crossed 2M in the last 30 minutes and is still climbing, treat it as an incident now.
