Yes, 2 million is a serious problem.

**What consumer lag means:** messages sit in a Kafka topic waiting to be processed. Lag is the count of unread messages between the latest produced message and the last one your consumer read.

**What 2 million means for users:** your consumers are far behind producers. Depending on your throughput, users experience:

- **Delayed data** — events, notifications, or state changes arrive late or not at all
- **Stale reads** — if downstream systems read from processed output, they see old data
- **Cascading failures** — if lag keeps climbing, consumers may fall behind the retention window and lose messages permanently

**Immediate questions to answer:**

1. Is lag still growing, or has it plateaued? Growing lag means consumers cannot keep up. A plateau means they are processing but behind.
2. What is your topic's retention period? If retention is 24 hours and lag represents more than 24 hours of data, you will lose messages.
3. Did throughput spike, or did consumers slow down? Check producer rate and consumer CPU/memory.

**Common fixes:** add consumer instances (if the topic has enough partitions), find and fix a slow processing step, or temporarily reduce message complexity upstream.

The number alone is not the full picture — lag of 2 million at 10 messages/second is catastrophic (55 hours behind); at 100,000 messages/second it is 20 seconds behind and recoverable.
