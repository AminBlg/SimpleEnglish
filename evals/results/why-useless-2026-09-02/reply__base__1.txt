**Yes, 2 million consumer lag is bad** — but how bad depends on your throughput rate.

## What it means

Consumer lag = the number of messages in a Kafka topic that have been *produced* but not yet *consumed*. Your consumers are falling behind producers by 2 million messages.

## Is 2M actually critical?

It depends on your **consumption rate**:

| Consumption rate | Time to clear 2M lag |
|---|---|
| 100k msg/sec | ~20 seconds — probably fine |
| 10k msg/sec | ~3 minutes — watch it |
| 1k msg/sec | ~33 minutes — likely a problem |
| 100 msg/sec | ~5.5 hours — serious incident |

If lag is *growing*, that's the real alarm — producers are outpacing consumers and the gap will keep widening.

## What users likely experience

- **Delayed data**: anything downstream of this consumer (dashboards, notifications, recommendations, search indexes) shows stale or missing data
- **Delayed events**: order confirmations, email triggers, fraud checks — anything event-driven is backed up
- **Eventually: data loss risk** — if lag grows past your topic's retention window (e.g., 7 days of data), oldest messages get deleted before they're consumed

## Immediate things to check

1. **Is lag growing or stable?** Stable high lag is a backlog; growing lag is an active incident.
2. **Are consumers healthy?** Check for crashed consumer instances, GC pauses, or slow downstream DB writes.
3. **Did a deploy just happen?** Consumer restarts cause temporary lag spikes.
4. **Is the consumer group running?** A stopped consumer group will show 100% of topic messages as lag.

## Quick remediation options

- **Scale out consumers** (add more instances up to the partition count)
- **Increase batch size / `max.poll.records`** if consumers are CPU-bound
- **Identify and fix the slow operation** (usually a slow DB write or external API call in the consumer loop)

The number alone isn't the verdict — the trend and your message rate are what determine severity.
