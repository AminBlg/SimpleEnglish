Yes, that is bad. Here is what it means.

**What consumer lag is:** the number of messages your consumer has not yet processed. A lag of 2 million means your consumers are 2 million messages behind the producers.

**What users experience depends on your system**, but common effects are:

- **Delayed data** — if your pipeline feeds a UI or API, users see stale information proportional to how long it takes to clear that backlog.
- **Missed time windows** — if consumers drive time-sensitive logic (alerts, fraud checks, order confirmations), those fire late or not at all.
- **Memory/disk pressure** — Kafka retains unprocessed messages on disk; if lag keeps growing, you risk hitting retention limits and losing messages permanently before they are processed.

**The key question is: is lag growing or stable?**

- Stable lag means consumers keep up with current traffic but never caught up from a past spike. You have time to investigate.
- Growing lag means consumers are slower than producers right now. This is urgent — the backlog will not resolve on its own.

**Immediate checks:**

1. Look at consumer CPU and memory — a slow consumer is often resource-starved.
2. Check if a consumer instance crashed or restarted — fewer consumers means more lag per remaining instance.
3. Check producer throughput for a sudden spike that outpaced your consumer capacity.

If lag is growing, scale up consumer instances or reduce processing time per message first, then investigate the root cause.
