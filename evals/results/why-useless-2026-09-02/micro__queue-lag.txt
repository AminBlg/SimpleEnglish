Consumer lag is the number of messages sitting in a Kafka topic that a consumer has not yet processed. A lag of 2 million means your consumers are 2 million messages behind producers, and whether that is bad depends entirely on your throughput rate and how quickly the gap is growing. If your consumers process 1 million messages per second and the lag is stable, you have roughly a 2-second delay, which may be acceptable. If the lag is climbing, consumers cannot keep up with production rate, and the gap will widen until you intervene or the system buckles.

For users, the practical impact maps directly to what those messages represent. If this is a real-time notification pipeline, users may receive alerts minutes or hours late. If it is an order-processing or payment stream, transactions may appear pending or delayed. If it is a read-model update pipeline (keeping a database in sync), users will see stale data.

The most important question to answer right now is whether the lag is stable, growing, or shrinking, because that tells you if you have a chronic capacity problem or an acute incident in progress.
