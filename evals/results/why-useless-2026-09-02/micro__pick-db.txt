Before sharding, add indexes on the columns you filter and sort by most often. Sharding is a last resort because it adds enormous operational complexity and breaks transactions that span shards. Run `EXPLAIN (ANALYZE, BUFFERS)` on your slowest queries to see whether they are doing sequential scans, which indexes would fix, or whether they are already index-scanning but returning too many rows. Also check whether you can add read replicas to offload SELECT traffic, which is far simpler than sharding and often sufficient at 40 million rows. Partitioning the table by a natural range like date is a good middle step if indexes and replicas are not enough, since it keeps everything in one logical table while letting Postgres prune irrelevant partitions at query time.
