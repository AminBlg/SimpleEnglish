Yes, 2 million messages of consumer lag is serious. Lag is the count of messages produced but not yet consumed — it means your consumers are falling behind producers by that many messages.

The user impact depends on what the consumer does. If it drives real-time features (notifications, live feeds, fraud checks, order processing), users experience delays proportional to your throughput rate. At 10,000 messages per second, 2 million messages means a 200-second backlog — over three minutes of stale data or delayed actions.

The root cause is almost always one of three things: a consumer that is too slow (CPU-bound processing, slow downstream calls), too few consumer instances relative to partition count, or a sudden traffic spike the current fleet cannot absorb. Check consumer CPU, downstream latency, and whether partition count matches or exceeds your consumer instance count.

To stop the bleed, scale consumer instances up to match partition count first, then profile the processing path for bottlenecks. If lag is still climbing after scaling, the problem is per-message processing time, not parallelism.
