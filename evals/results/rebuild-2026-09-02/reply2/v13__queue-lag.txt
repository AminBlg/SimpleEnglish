**Yes, 2 million lag is serious — but context determines severity.**

## What it means

Consumer lag is the gap between the latest message produced and the latest message your consumer has processed. At 2 million, your consumers are 2 million messages behind the producers.

## Whether it's bad depends on three things

**1. Throughput rate.** At 100k msg/s, 2M lag = 20 seconds behind. At 1k msg/s, it's 33 minutes. The lag number alone tells you nothing without the rate.

**2. Trend.** Lag that is stable (consumers keeping pace but offset is high) is a different problem from lag that is climbing. Climbing lag means consumers cannot keep up — that is the dangerous case.

**3. What the topic drives.** A lag on an analytics pipeline is tolerable. A lag on a payment processing or notification topic means users are waiting or missing events.

## User impact (likely scenarios)

- **Notifications/emails delayed** — users act on stale state or miss time-sensitive alerts.
- **Order/payment processing backed up** — transactions appear to hang.
- **Search indexes or caches stale** — users see outdated data.
- **Event-driven workflows frozen** — anything downstream of the consumer stops progressing.

## Immediate checks

1. `kafka-consumer-groups.sh --describe` — confirm the lag is growing, not steady.
2. Check consumer CPU and GC — a slow consumer is often the cause.
3. Check producer rate — a traffic spike may have outpaced a healthy consumer.
4. Check for consumer rebalancing — frequent rebalances stall processing.

## Fast mitigations

- **Scale out consumers** (add partitions first if you are at partition count already).
- **Increase `fetch.min.bytes` / `max.poll.records`** to batch more per poll.
- **Check for a stuck partition** — sometimes one partition holds nearly all the lag.

The climbing trend is the part to fix urgently. Stable high lag can sometimes be caught up overnight; climbing lag compounds.
