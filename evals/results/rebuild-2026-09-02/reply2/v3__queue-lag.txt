Consumer lag of 2 million means your consumers are 2 million messages behind the producers. Whether that is bad depends entirely on your message rate and your expected processing time.

If your topic produces 1 million messages per second, a 2-million lag means you are about 2 seconds behind — likely fine. If it produces 1,000 messages per second, you are 33 minutes behind — almost certainly a problem. The lag number alone is meaningless without the production rate alongside it.

For users, the practical effect is delayed data. If your pipeline feeds a search index, a recommendation engine, or a notifications system, users see stale results or late alerts proportional to how far behind you are. If the pipeline feeds real-time analytics or fraud detection, a multi-minute lag can mean missed signals.

The more urgent question is whether the lag is growing, stable, or shrinking. A stable lag at 2 million means your consumers are keeping pace but never catching up — you need more consumer capacity. A growing lag means consumers cannot keep up at all and will eventually cause memory or disk pressure on the brokers if retention limits are hit.

Check consumer CPU and network saturation, then look at whether adding partitions and consumer instances closes the gap.
