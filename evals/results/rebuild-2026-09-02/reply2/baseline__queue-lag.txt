Yes, 2 million consumer lag is bad — but how bad depends on your throughput rate.

## What it means

Consumer lag is the number of messages sitting in Kafka that your consumers haven't processed yet. At 2M lag, your consumers are behind producers by 2 million messages.

**The critical metric is time-to-drain, not the raw number:**
- If you produce 100k msg/s and consume 110k msg/s → ~18s to drain, probably fine
- If you produce 100k msg/s and consume 100k msg/s → lag is stable but never drains, growing problem
- If lag is *climbing*, consumers are slower than producers — that's the urgent case

## Impact on users

Depends entirely on what those consumers do:

| Consumer purpose | User impact at 2M lag |
|---|---|
| Real-time notifications / events | Delayed by minutes to hours |
| Order processing / payments | Transactions stuck in queue |
| Analytics / dashboards | Stale data shown to users |
| Email/SMS delivery | Delayed communications |
| Search index updates | Stale search results |
| Audit/logging (async) | Probably none immediately |

## Immediate questions to answer

1. **Is lag growing or stable?** Growing = active incident. Stable = capacity problem.
2. **What's the consumer group?** Some groups matter more than others.
3. **Why are consumers slow?** Check: consumer CPU/memory, downstream DB latency, GC pauses, or consumers crashed entirely (lag grows because there's nobody consuming).
4. **Is retention at risk?** If messages age out before consumers reach them, data is permanently lost.

If lag is actively climbing and the consumer handles user-facing workflows, treat it as an incident now. The fix is usually either scaling consumer instances or fixing the downstream bottleneck causing slow processing.
