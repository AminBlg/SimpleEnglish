Redis is caching the price data with a TTL (time-to-live) of at least 10 minutes, so reads hit the cache instead of the database until that TTL expires. The fix depends on your write path: when a price update is written to the database, also invalidate (or update) the corresponding Redis key immediately, so the next read fetches fresh data. The cleanest approach is cache-aside with proactive invalidation — call `DEL price:<id>` (or `SET price:<id> <new_value>`) in the same transaction or immediately after the database write commits.
