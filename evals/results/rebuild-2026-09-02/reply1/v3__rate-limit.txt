A token bucket works like a physical bucket that refills at a fixed rate. Each user has a bucket that holds up to 100 tokens; one token is consumed per request, and the bucket gains tokens at 100 per minute (roughly 1.67 per second). If the bucket is full, new tokens overflow and are discarded. A request that arrives when the bucket is empty is rejected or queued.

This fits your requirement well. It enforces the 100-requests-per-minute ceiling while naturally allowing short bursts (a user who was idle for 30 seconds can fire 50 requests instantly), which feels fair and accommodates legitimate traffic spikes without extra configuration. The main alternative, a fixed window counter, is simpler but lets a user fire 100 requests at the end of one window and 100 more at the start of the next, effectively doubling the rate at the boundary.
