**Yes, 2 million consumer lag is bad.** Here's what it means:

## What the number means

Consumer lag is the count of messages sitting in Kafka partitions that your consumers have not yet processed. At 2 million, your consumers are falling behind producers — messages are arriving faster than they are being consumed.

## Why it matters for users

The impact depends entirely on what that consumer processes:

| Consumer role | User-visible effect |
|---|---|
| Sends emails / notifications | Users receive them minutes or hours late |
| Updates search indexes | Search results show stale data |
| Processes payments / orders | Transactions appear pending longer than expected |
| Writes to a read database | Reads return outdated records |
| Feeds analytics / dashboards | Metrics are delayed or frozen |

## Immediate questions to answer

1. **Is lag growing or stable?** A stable 2M is a chronic backlog. A growing 2M is an active incident — consumers will never catch up without intervention.
2. **What is your message rate?** 2M lag at 10k msg/s = 3 minutes behind. At 1k msg/s = 33 minutes behind.
3. **Did producer throughput spike, or did consumers slow down?** Check both sides. A consumer crash, GC pause, or downstream DB bottleneck is common.

## What to do right now

- Check consumer group lag per partition: `kafka-consumer-groups.sh --describe --group <your-group>`
- Look for dead or slow consumer instances in your consumer group
- If lag is growing, scale out consumers (add instances up to the partition count)
- If one partition holds all the lag, you have a hot partition — repartitioning or a key strategy fix is needed

The number alone is not the whole story, but 2M warrants immediate investigation regardless of your throughput.
